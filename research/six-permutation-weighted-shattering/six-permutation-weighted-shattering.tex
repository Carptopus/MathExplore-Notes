\begin{abstract}
Let $F_3(n)$ be the maximum number of triples of an $n$-element set that can
be shattered by six permutations, and let
$c_3=\lim_{n\to\infty}F_3(n)/\binom n3$.  \v{C}ern\'a, Kielak and Volec
recently proved
\[
\frac{482}{975}\le c_3\le\frac12.
\]
We give a weighted recursive construction that applies to every finite
six-permutation template.  If $G$ is the set of triples shattered by a
template on $[N]$, and $w_1,\ldots,w_N$ is a probability vector with at least
two positive entries, put
\[
A=6\sum_{\{i,j,k\}\in G}w_iw_jw_k,
\qquad B=\sum_{i=1}^Nw_i^3.
\]
We prove that $c_3\ge A/(1-B)$.  Uniform weights recover the standard
iterated blow-up bound.  Applying the weighted construction to the published
26-point template of \v{C}ern\'a, Kielak and Volec gives
\[
\boxed{c_3\ge\frac{1288385}{2599242}
=0.495677201276\ldots}.
\]
The proof uses independent infinite words to define six lexicographic orders;
conditioning on their first letters gives the renewal identity $D=A+BD$.
All arithmetic for the 26-point application is verified by an accompanying
exact certificate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A family of permutations of a finite set \emph{shatters} a subset when every
relative ordering of that subset occurs in the family.  This is a permutation
analogue of classical shattering in extremal set theory.  Johnson and Wickes
introduced the problem of maximizing the number of $k$-sets shattered by a
fixed number of permutations~\cite{johnson-wickes}.  In the first nontrivial
complete case, six permutations attempt to realize all $3!=6$ orders on as
many triples as possible.

Write $F_3(n)$ for the maximum number of triples of $[n]$ shattered by six
permutations, and put
\[
f_3(n)=\frac{F_3(n)}{\binom n3}.
\]
Johnson and Wickes proved that the limit
\[
c_3=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_3(n)
\]
exists and developed a finite-template recursion.  \v{C}ern\'a, Kielak and
Volec subsequently used flag algebras for the upper bound and a 26-point
recursive construction for the lower bound
\[
\frac{482}{975}\le c_3\le\frac12
\]
\cite{cerna-kielak-volec}.  Their lower construction iterates the finite
template uniformly.

We allow the template classes to have unequal asymptotic masses.  The main
result is a weighted recursion theorem for an arbitrary finite template.  Its
proof uses independent infinite words over the template alphabet.  Each of
the six template permutations defines a lexicographic order on the words.
For three sampled words, either their first letters form a shattered template
triple, all three first letters coincide and the problem restarts on the
tails, or the triple cannot be shattered.  This gives an exact renewal
equation.

For the 26-point template in \cite{cerna-kielak-volec}, a rational weight
vector improves the published uniform bound from
$482/975=0.494358974359\ldots$ to
$1288385/2599242=0.495677201276\ldots$.  The general weighted theorem is the
structural part of the result; the numerical bound is an exact application to
the strongest publicly available finite template that we could verify.

The new lower bound remains below $1/2$, and no equality or matching upper
bound is claimed.  The authors of \cite{cerna-kielak-volec} continue to work
on this problem, so we compare only with their accessible published
construction.

\section{Templates and weighted lexicographic recursion}

Let
\[
\Pi=(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_6)
\]
be six rank-map permutations of $[N]$.  We write
$a<_{\pi_r}b$ if and only if $\pi_r(a)<\pi_r(b)$.  A triple
$T=\{i,j,k\}\subset[N]$ is \emph{shattered by} $\Pi$ if its restrictions in
the six induced orders realize all six linear orders of $T$.  Denote the set of shattered triples by
$\shat(\Pi)$.

Let $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_N)$ satisfy
\[
w_i\ge0,
\qquad \sum_{i=1}^Nw_i=1,
\]
and suppose at least two $w_i$ are positive.  Define
\[
A(\Pi,w)=6\sum_{\{i,j,k\}\in\shat(\Pi)}w_iw_jw_k,
\qquad
B(w)=\sum_{i=1}^Nw_i^3.
\]
The first quantity is the probability that three independent $w$-letters
are pairwise distinct and form a shattered template triple.  The second is
the probability that all three letters coincide.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted recursion]\label{thm:weighted}
For every $n\ge3$, there exist six permutations of $[n]$ that shatter at
least
\[
\frac{A(\Pi,w)}{1-B(w)}\binom n3
\]
triples.  Consequently,
\[
\boxed{c_3\ge\frac{A(\Pi,w)}{1-B(w)}}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent infinite words over $[N]$, with every
letter in every coordinate independently distributed according to $w$.  For
each $r\in[6]$, order the alphabet $[N]$ by $\pi_r$, and order the words
lexicographically using this alphabet order.

For two independent words, the probability that their first $m$ letters
agree is
\[
\left(\sum_{i=1}^Nw_i^2\right)^m.
\]
At least two weights are positive, so $\sum_iw_i^2<1$.  Hence two infinite
words coincide with probability zero.  Since only finitely many words are
sampled, almost surely every lexicographic order is a permutation of the
sample indices.

Fix three sample indices, and let $D$ be the probability that their triple is
shattered by the six lexicographic orders.  Classify the three first letters.
If they are pairwise distinct and their unordered set lies in $\shat(\Pi)$,
the template realizes all six relative orders.  This event has probability
$A(\Pi,w)$.

If all three first letters are equal, their relative orders are determined by
the independent tails.  Conditional on this event, the probability of being
shattered is again $D$.  The event has probability $B(w)$.

If exactly two first letters agree, the word with the different first letter
lies on the same side of the other two whenever the relative order of the two
alphabet letters is fixed.  Thus all six orders cannot occur.  If the first
letters are pairwise distinct but their template triple is not shattered,
later coordinates cannot change their relative order, so the sampled triple
again fails.

The cases are exhaustive, and therefore
\[
D=A(\Pi,w)+B(w)D.
\]
Since at least two weights are positive, $B(w)<1$, and hence
\[
D=\frac{A(\Pi,w)}{1-B(w)}.
\]
Every triple of sample indices has the same probability $D$ of being
shattered.  The expected number of shattered triples is therefore
$D\binom n3$, so at least one finite sample attains at least this value.  If
$D>0$, any six orders shattering a triple are necessarily distinct.  If
$D=0$, choose any six distinct permutations, which is possible for $n\ge3$.
This proves the finite statement, and taking the limiting maximum gives the
asserted bound on $c_3$.
\end{proof}

\section{Uniform recursion as a special case}

Suppose $w_i=1/N$ for every $i$, and let $s=|\shat(\Pi)|$.  Then
\[
A=\frac{6s}{N^3},
\qquad
B=\frac1{N^2},
\]
and Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted} becomes
\[
c_3\ge\frac{6s}{N(N^2-1)}.
\]
This is the standard uniform finite-template bound used in
\cite{johnson-wickes,cerna-kielak-volec}.  Thus the weighted theorem extends
the known recursion rather than replacing it with a different limiting
object.

For a fixed template, the theorem gives the finite-dimensional optimization
problem
\[
\Phi_{\Pi}(w)
=\frac{6\sum_{\{i,j,k\}\in\shat(\Pi)}w_iw_jw_k}
{1-\sum_iw_i^3}
\]
over the probability simplex.  Numerical optimization can suggest a weight
vector, but every claimed lower bound can be certified with rational weights
and exact arithmetic.

\section{The 26-point certificate}

\v{C}ern\'a, Kielak and Volec give six permutations of $[26]$ that shatter
1446 of the 2600 triples~\cite[Table~2]{cerna-kielak-volec}.  Uniform
recursion yields
\[
\frac{6\cdot1446}{26(26^2-1)}=\frac{482}{975}.
\]
For the same template, take $w_i=q_i/250$, where
\[
\begin{aligned}
(q_1,\ldots,q_{26})={}&(12,7,7,8,9,14,9,9,9,16,16,9,12,\\
&10,6,6,8,7,7,12,8,14,8,9,9,9).
\end{aligned}
\]
The entries are positive integers with sum 250.  Exact summation over the
1446 shattered triples gives
\[
A=\frac{773031}{1562500},
\qquad
B=\frac{7387}{3906250}.
\]

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:new-bound}
The limiting proportion of triples shattered by six permutations satisfies
\[
\boxed{
c_3\ge\frac{1288385}{2599242}
=0.495677201276\ldots }.
\]
\end{corollary}

The improvement over the published uniform construction is exact:
\[
\frac{1288385}{2599242}-\frac{482}{975}
=\frac{1113577}{844753650}
=0.001318226917\ldots>0.
\]

The accompanying checker stores the six rank maps printed in
\cite{cerna-kielak-volec}, enumerates all $\binom{26}{3}=2600$ triples,
confirms the count 1446, and evaluates $A$, $B$, the weighted density and the
difference above using rational arithmetic.  It also converts the rank-map
convention to equivalent order lists and checks that both representations
give the same shattered triples.  This conversion is an implementation
cross-check, not an independent source for the template.

\section{Scope and reproducibility}

The result supplies a general weighted construction and a strict improvement
of the lower bound obtained from the accessible published 26-point template.
It does not prove $c_3=1/2$, classify optimal templates, or improve the flag
algebra upper bound in \cite{cerna-kielak-volec}.  Continuous optimization was
used only to locate the displayed integer vector; the theorem and final
numerical comparison are exact.

Our literature check covered the journal and arXiv versions of
\cite{johnson-wickes}, the EUROCOMB'25 extended abstract
\cite{cerna-kielak-volec}, the authors' public publication pages, and direct
searches for weighted blow-ups and the exact constant.  The accessible texts
use uniform recursion and do not state Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted} or
Corollary~\ref{cor:new-bound}.  This records the checked boundary; it is not a
claim that unpublished or unindexed parallel work does not exist.

The verification script is supplementary evidence for the finite template
and arithmetic.  The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted} does not depend on
the script.
