## Main result

Let $X$ be a well-ordered rooted tree with root $r$. For every point $a$
covering $r$, put $Q_a=\uparrow a$. The manuscript proves that $X$ admits a
bi-Esakia topology whenever every $Q_a$ is finite and, for some fixed finite
co-tree comb $C_n$, no order dual $Q_a^{\mathrm{op}}$ contains $C_n$ as an
order-embedded subposet.

The class allows arbitrary root width, with no uniform bound on crown size or
depth. The uniform restriction is the omission of one fixed finite comb.

## Proof structure

- A finite-anchor synchronization theorem treats arbitrarily many finite
  posets amalgamated over a common finite downset.
- Stem-exact surjective bi-p-morphisms organize nonisomorphic petals into
  finitely many actual anchor types.
- The synchronized topology is also identified with the inverse limit of the
  finite-petal quotients.
- Martins' better-partial-order theorem for comb-bounded finite co-trees
  supplies the required finite family of actual minimal anchors.

## Scope and prior-work boundary

The candidate contribution is the stem-exact finite-anchor synchronization
criterion and its application to comb-bounded crowns of well-ordered rooted
trees. General inverse-limit closure, synchronization of isomorphic finite
pieces, the comb-bounded better-partial-order theorem, and the known infinite
bi-p-image antichain are prior work and are cited in the manuscript.

This is a sufficient theorem, not a classification. It does not prove that the
comb condition is necessary, characterize all well-ordered trees or forests,
or turn the known infinite antichain into a nonrepresentability theorem.
Absolute priority remains qualified pending broader public and expert review.

The result is theoretical; no finite computation or software output is used
to establish the theorem.
