\begin{abstract}
Let $G=G_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup G_k$ be a finite simple graph with $k$ nonempty
connected components and $n$ vertices. For $d\geq2$, let $\Delta_d^t(G)$ be
the total $d$-cut complex. We prove that if $k\geq d$ and, for every component
$G_i$ and every $2\leq r\leq d$, the complex $\Delta_r^t(G_i)$ is void or
integer-acyclic, then
\[
\Delta_d^t(G)\simeq
\bigvee_{\binom{k-1}{d-1}}S^{n-d-1}.
\]
This extends the author's earlier low-component preprint~\cite{ref8}, which answered
Question 29 of Carnero Bravo under the written hypotheses of the source
theorem. The present result also answers Question 30 for the stated component
class: it removes the simple-connectivity condition on the component clique
complexes and weakens component contractibility to integer acyclicity
throughout all $k\geq d$. The homology calculation replaces an
objectwise-contractible homotopy-colimit argument by its integral homology
spectral sequence. The remaining homotopy problem is closed by direct
connectivity arguments. The most delicate layer, $k=d$, is handled by a
weak-composition cover whose nonbaseline blocks are cones and whose relevant
intersections are connected. The acyclicity assumption cannot simply be
deleted: for $G=C_4\sqcup K_1$ and $d=2$, the total cut complex has an
additional first homology class.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For a graph $G$ and an integer $d\geq1$, the total $d$-cut complex is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d^t(G)
=\{\sigma\subseteq V(G):\alpha(G-\sigma)\geq d\}.
\label{eq:total-cut}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, a vertex set is a face precisely when its complement contains
an independent set of size $d$. Total cut complexes were introduced by Bayer,
Denker, Jeli\'c Milutinovi\'c, Rowlands, Sundaram, and Xue \cite{ref1}. Their
topology is closely related to the bounded-independence complexes
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BI}_d(G)
=\{\sigma\subseteq V(G):\alpha(G[\sigma])<d\},
\label{eq:bounded-independence}
\end{equation}
because $\Delta_d^t(G)$ and $\mathrm{BI}_d(G)$ are combinatorial Alexander
duals.

Carnero Bravo \cite{ref2} developed homotopy-colimit decompositions for these
filtrations. In particular, \cite[Theorem 28]{ref2} proves the wedge formula
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d^t(G)\simeq
\bigvee_{\binom{k-1}{d-1}}S^{n-d-1}
\label{eq:wedge-formula}
\end{equation}
when $G$ has $k\geq d+3$ connected components, each component total-cut
complex in the relevant range is void or contractible, and each component
clique complex $\mathrm{BI}_2(G_i)$ is simply connected. The two questions
immediately following that theorem ask whether \eqref{eq:wedge-formula} also
holds for $k=d,d+1,d+2$ under the same hypotheses \cite[Question 29]{ref2},
and whether the clique-complex connectivity condition can be removed
\cite[Question 30]{ref2}.

The author's earlier preprint \cite{ref8} answered Question 29 under the
written void-or-contractible and clique-complex hypotheses of
\cite[Theorem 28]{ref2}. The present manuscript subsumes that result and
resolves Question 30 on a slightly larger component class. Its new content
relative to \cite{ref8} is the acyclic composition-diagram argument, the
corresponding removal of the component clique-complex condition, the $k=d$
connected-intersection mechanism under the weaker hypothesis, and the
negative control in Section~\ref{sec:counterexample}. If released, this
manuscript is intended as a successor to \cite{ref8}, not as an unrelated
claim of first priority for the three low-component layers.

\begin{maintheorem}
Let $d\geq2$, and let
\[
G=G_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup G_k
\]
be a finite simple graph with $k\geq d$ nonempty connected components and
$n$ vertices. Suppose that, for every $i$ and every $2\leq r\leq d$, the
complex $\Delta_r^t(G_i)$ is either void or integer-acyclic. Then
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d^t(G)\simeq
\bigvee_{\binom{k-1}{d-1}}S^{n-d-1}.
\label{eq:main-theorem}
\end{equation}
\end{maintheorem}

Here integer-acyclic means nonvoid with all reduced integral homology groups
zero. Thus every contractible complex is integer-acyclic, but no
contractibility or fundamental-group condition is assumed.

There are two separate issues in the proof. First, the composition-poset
diagram of \cite{ref2} still determines integral homology when its objects are
merely integer-acyclic. The homology spectral sequence of the homotopy colimit
collapses to the homology of the composition poset. Alexander duality then
gives the homology of \eqref{eq:main-theorem}. Second, this homology must be
upgraded to a homotopy equivalence. For $k\geq d+1$, the required simple
connectivity is purely combinatorial and does not use any component
clique-complex condition. For $k=d$, the complete-$2$-skeleton method is
unavailable; a weak-composition cover and a van Kampen attachment argument
replace it.

Theorem A formally weakens the written hypotheses of \cite[Theorem 28]{ref2},
but we do not claim that integer acyclicity is strictly more general within
every natural graph family. A finite search conducted during discovery found
no small component total-cut complex that was integer-acyclic but
noncontractible. That search is not part of the proof. On the other hand,
Section~\ref{sec:counterexample} gives a small counterexample showing that the
acyclicity hypothesis cannot be removed altogether.

\section{Definitions and conventions}\label{sec:definitions}

All graphs and simplicial complexes are finite. A complex $K$ is taken on a
fixed finite ambient ground set $\Omega$ and may have ghost vertices, meaning
elements of $\Omega$ whose singleton is not a face. We distinguish the void
complex on $\Omega$, which has no faces, from $\{\varnothing\}$. We use
$\{\varnothing\}=S^{-1}$ as the identity for joins. The combinatorial
Alexander dual of $K$ relative to $\Omega$, where $N=|\Omega|$, is
\begin{equation}
K^*=\{\sigma\subseteq\Omega:\Omega-\sigma\notin K\}.
\label{eq:alexander-dual}
\end{equation}
Combinatorial Alexander duality gives \cite{ref3}
\begin{equation}
\widetilde H_j(K;\mathbb Z)
\cong
\widetilde H^{N-j-3}(K^*;\mathbb Z).
\label{eq:combinatorial-ad}
\end{equation}

The complexes $\Delta_r^t(H)$ and $\mathrm{BI}_r(H)$ are both taken on the
fixed ground set $V(H)$. Definitions \eqref{eq:total-cut} and
\eqref{eq:bounded-independence} immediately imply
\begin{equation}
\Delta_r^t(H)=\mathrm{BI}_r(H)^*.
\label{eq:dual-filtrations}
\end{equation}
For weak-composition formulas we extend the total-cut filtration by
\begin{equation}
\Delta_0^t(H)=\Delta^{V(H)},
\qquad
\Delta_1^t(H)=\partial\Delta^{V(H)}.
\label{eq:total-cut-extension}
\end{equation}
The second equality holds because $H-\sigma$ contains an independent vertex
if and only if $\sigma\neq V(H)$. For bounded-independence joins we set
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BI}_1(H)=\{\varnothing\}.
\label{eq:bi-one}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:remaining-components}
If deleting a vertex set $\sigma$ leaves at least $d$ nonempty connected
components of $G$, then $\sigma\in\Delta_d^t(G)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose one remaining vertex from each of $d$ components. Vertices in distinct
components are pairwise nonadjacent.
\end{proof}

We also use the standard fact that a simply connected CW complex whose only
nonzero reduced integral homology is $\mathbb Z^b$ in degree $q\geq2$ is
homotopy equivalent to a wedge of $b$ copies of $S^q$ \cite{ref4}.

\section{The acyclic composition diagram}\label{sec:composition}

This section computes the integral homology of both dual complexes throughout
the range $k\geq d$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:component-bi-acyclic}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem A, for every $i$ and $2\leq r\leq d$, the
complex $\mathrm{BI}_r(G_i)$ is nonempty and integer-acyclic.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\Delta_r^t(G_i)$ is void, its Alexander dual is the full simplex on
$V(G_i)$. Suppose instead that $\Delta_r^t(G_i)$ is integer-acyclic. The
integral universal coefficient theorem gives vanishing reduced integral
cohomology, and \eqref{eq:combinatorial-ad} then gives vanishing reduced
integral homology for $\mathrm{BI}_r(G_i)$. Since $r\geq2$, the empty face and
every singleton lie in $\mathrm{BI}_r(G_i)$, so it is nonempty.
\end{proof}

Let $m=d+k-1$. Following \cite{ref2}, define the finite poset
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_{m,k}
=\bigcup_{s=k+1}^{m}
\{(c_1,\ldots,c_k)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}^k:
c_1+\cdots+c_k=s\},
\label{eq:composition-poset}
\end{equation}
ordered coordinatewise. For $\mathbf c\in\mathcal C_{m,k}$, put
\begin{equation}
X_{\mathbf c}
=\mathrm{BI}_{c_1}(G_1)*\cdots*\mathrm{BI}_{c_k}(G_k).
\label{eq:composition-object}
\end{equation}
Every coordinate satisfies $1\leq c_i\leq d$. Moreover, at least one
coordinate is at least $2$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:diagram-objects}
Every $X_{\mathbf c}$ is nonempty, connected, and integer-acyclic.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a coordinate $i$ with $c_i\geq2$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:component-bi-acyclic}, the corresponding factor is nonempty and
integer-acyclic. Factors with $c_j=1$ are join identities; all other genuine
factors are nonempty. The reduced chain complex of a finite join satisfies the
standard tensor--Tor join formula. A join with one integer-acyclic genuine
factor therefore has zero reduced integral homology. It has vertices, so
vanishing reduced $H_0$ also says that it is connected.
\end{proof}

The composition decomposition of \cite[Lemma 25 and Corollary 27]{ref2} gives
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BI}_d(G)
=\operatorname*{colim}_{\mathcal C_{m,k}}X_{\mathbf c}
\simeq
\operatorname*{hocolim}_{\mathcal C_{m,k}}X_{\mathbf c}.
\label{eq:hocolim-model}
\end{equation}
The second equivalence is the standard homotopy-colimit comparison for this
finite diagram of subcomplex inclusions; the diagram includes all its
nonempty finite intersections.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bi-homology}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem A,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde H_j(\mathrm{BI}_d(G);\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{\binom{k-1}{d-1}},&j=d-2,\\
0,&j\neq d-2.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bi-homology}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The integral homology spectral sequence for the homotopy colimit in
\eqref{eq:hocolim-model} has
\begin{equation}
E^2_{p,q}
=H_p(\mathcal C_{m,k};\mathcal H_q(X))
\Longrightarrow
H_{p+q}(\operatorname*{hocolim}X;\mathbb Z).
\label{eq:hocolim-ss}
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:diagram-objects}, $\mathcal H_q(X)=0$ for $q>0$. The $H_0$
coefficient system is the constant system $\mathbb Z$: every object is
nonempty and connected, and every structure inclusion induces the identity
between its canonical $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$. Thus \eqref{eq:hocolim-ss} has only
its $q=0$ row and yields
\begin{equation}
H_*(\mathrm{BI}_d(G);\mathbb Z)
\cong H_*(\Delta(\mathcal C_{m,k});\mathbb Z).
\label{eq:poset-homology}
\end{equation}
By \cite[Proposition 12]{ref2}, for $k\geq d$ the order complex is homotopy
equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\Delta(\mathcal C_{d+k-1,k})
\simeq
\bigvee_{\binom{k-1}{d-1}}S^{d-2}.
\label{eq:composition-wedge}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:poset-homology} and \eqref{eq:composition-wedge} prove the
claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:total-cut-homology}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem A,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_d^t(G);\mathbb Z)
\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{\binom{k-1}{d-1}},&j=n-d-1,\\
0,&j\neq n-d-1.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:total-cut-homology}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply combinatorial Alexander duality to \eqref{eq:bi-homology}. Since the
single nonzero group is free, the integral universal coefficient theorem
introduces neither hidden torsion nor extension terms. If $n=k=d$, the target
degree is $-1$ and the statement follows directly from
$\Delta_d^t(dK_1)=\{\varnothing\}=S^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Connectivity for \texorpdfstring{$k\geq d+1$}{k >= d+1}}\label{sec:connectivity}

Put
\begin{equation}
q=n-d-1.
\label{eq:q}
\end{equation}
Every face of $\Delta_d^t(G)$ has at most $n-d$ vertices, because its
complement contains an independent $d$-set. Hence
\begin{equation}
\dim\Delta_d^t(G)\leq q.
\label{eq:dimension-bound}
\end{equation}
The low-dimensional cases can therefore be read directly from
\eqref{eq:total-cut-homology}: if $q=0$, the complex is a set of
$\binom{k-1}{d-1}+1$ points; if $q=1$, it is a connected graph with cycle rank
$\binom{k-1}{d-1}$ and hence the required wedge of circles. We now establish
simple connectivity whenever $q\geq2$.

\begin{proposition}[The layer $k=d+1$]\label{prop:k-d-plus-one}
If $k=d+1$, then
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d^t(G)\simeq\bigvee_d S^{n-d-1}.
\label{eq:k-d-plus-one}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $e=n-k=q$. The cases $e=0,1$ are the low-dimensional cases just
described. For completeness, when $e=0$ the complex consists of $d+1$ points.
When $e=1$, one component is a copy of $K_2$ and all others are singletons;
the complex is obtained from $K_{2,d}$ by adding the edge inside the $K_2$,
and its cycle rank is $d$.

Assume $e\geq2$. Choose a component $C$ as follows: if some component has at
least three vertices, take such a component; otherwise take any nonsingleton
component. Fix $a\in C$. For each other vertex $x$, deleting $a$ and $x$
empties at most one component. Lemma~\ref{lem:remaining-components} shows that
$ax$ is an edge of $\Delta_d^t(G)$. The edges from $a$ form a spanning tree of
the $1$-skeleton, so the fundamental group is generated by the triangular
loops
\begin{equation}
a-x-y-a
\label{eq:triangle-generator}
\end{equation}
for edges $xy$ outside that tree.

Suppose first that $|C|\geq3$. Choose a neighbor $b$ of $a$ in the connected
graph $C$. If $axy$ is a face, \eqref{eq:triangle-generator} is filled.
Otherwise $b\notin\{x,y\}$. The triples $abx$ and $aby$ are faces because
deleting either triple empties at most one component. Since $xy$ is a face,
choose an independent $d$-set $S$ in $G-\{x,y\}$. The failure of $axy$ forces
$a\in S$. Since $ab$ is an edge of $G$, $b\notin S$, so $S$ also witnesses
that $bxy$ is a face. The three triangles $abx$, $aby$, and $bxy$ form a disk
with boundary \eqref{eq:triangle-generator}.

It remains to consider the case in which every nonsingleton component is
$K_2$. Since $e\geq2$, there are at least two such components. Write one as
$\{a,b\}$ and choose a vertex $c$ in another. If $axy$ is not a face while
$xy$ is an edge, then $\{x,y\}=\{b,s\}$ for a singleton component $s$.
Indeed, without $b$ the deletion cannot both destroy the $a$-component and
leave too few other components, while with $b$ the remaining vertex must
exhaust a singleton. In this remaining case, $abc$, $acs$, and $bcs$ are
faces and form a disk with boundary $a-b-s-a$.

Every generator \eqref{eq:triangle-generator} is null-homotopic. Thus the
complex is simply connected. Corollary~\ref{cor:total-cut-homology} and the
wedge criterion complete the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The layer $k=d+2$]\label{prop:k-d-plus-two}
If $k=d+2$, then
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d^t(G)\simeq
\bigvee_{\binom{d+1}{2}}S^{n-d-1}.
\label{eq:k-d-plus-two}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Deleting two vertices empties at most two components, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:remaining-components} gives a complete $1$-skeleton. If every
component is a singleton, the complex is the complete graph on $d+2$ vertices
and has cycle rank $\binom{d+1}{2}$.

Otherwise fix a vertex $v$ in a component having at least two vertices. For
any two other vertices $x,y$, the deletion of $v,x,y$ cannot empty three
components: emptying the component of $v$ consumes one of $x,y$, leaving only
one vertex to empty another component. Therefore every triangle $vxy$ is a
face. Each loop in the complete $1$-skeleton is filled by a triangular fan
with apex $v$. The complex is simply connected, and
\eqref{eq:k-d-plus-two} follows from
Corollary~\ref{cor:total-cut-homology} and the wedge criterion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The range $k\geq d+3$]\label{prop:high-k}
If $k\geq d+3$, then
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d^t(G)\simeq
\bigvee_{\binom{k-1}{d-1}}S^{n-d-1}.
\label{eq:high-k}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Deleting any three vertices empties at most three components, leaving at least
$d$ nonempty components. Hence every three vertices form a face by
Lemma~\ref{lem:remaining-components}. The complex contains the full
$2$-skeleton of the simplex on $V(G)$ and is therefore simply connected. Since
$q\geq2$, Corollary~\ref{cor:total-cut-homology} and the wedge criterion give
\eqref{eq:high-k}.
\end{proof}

The point of Propositions~\ref{prop:k-d-plus-one}--\ref{prop:high-k} is that no
component topology enters the connectivity argument. All component hypotheses
were already consumed by the homology calculation in
Section~\ref{sec:composition}.

\section{The boundary layer \texorpdfstring{$k=d$}{k=d}}\label{sec:k-equals-d}

When the number of components equals $d$, the target multiplicity is one, but
the $1$- and $2$-skeleta need not be complete. We use all distributions of an
independent $d$-set among the components.

Let
\begin{equation}
W_d=\{\mathbf r=(r_1,\ldots,r_d)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^d:
r_1+\cdots+r_d=d\}
\label{eq:weak-compositions}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
X_{\mathbf r}
=\Delta_{r_1}^t(G_1)*\cdots*\Delta_{r_d}^t(G_d).
\label{eq:weak-composition-block}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:weak-composition-cover}
For $k=d$,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_d^t(G)=\bigcup_{\mathbf r\in W_d}X_{\mathbf r}.
\label{eq:weak-composition-cover}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\sigma\in\Delta_d^t(G)$, choose an independent $d$-set
$S\subseteq V(G)-\sigma$ and put $r_i=|S\cap V(G_i)|$. Then
$\mathbf r\in W_d$ and the part of $\sigma$ in $V(G_i)$ lies in
$\Delta_{r_i}^t(G_i)$. Hence $\sigma\in X_{\mathbf r}$. Conversely, if
$\sigma\in X_{\mathbf r}$, choose an independent $r_i$-set in each
$G_i-(\sigma\cap V(G_i))$; their union witnesses
$\sigma\in\Delta_d^t(G)$.
\end{proof}

The all-ones block is
\begin{equation}
B=X_{(1,\ldots,1)}
=\partial\Delta^{V(G_1)}*\cdots*\partial\Delta^{V(G_d)}
\cong S^q.
\label{eq:baseline-sphere}
\end{equation}
Every other weak composition has both a zero coordinate and a coordinate at
least $2$. After void blocks are omitted, the zero coordinate supplies a full
simplex factor, so every nonbaseline block is a cone.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:connected-traces}
Let $T$ be a nonempty finite set of nonbaseline weak compositions, and put
\[
m_i=\max_{\mathbf r\in T}r_i.
\]
If
\[
Z_T=\bigcap_{\mathbf r\in T}X_{\mathbf r}
\]
is nonvoid, then both $Z_T$ and $B\cap Z_T$ have nonempty path-connected
geometric realizations. Moreover, $Z_T$ is nonvoid if and only if
$B\cap Z_T$ is nonvoid.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The total-cut filtration decreases with its index, so
\begin{equation}
Z_T
=\Delta_{m_1}^t(G_1)*\cdots*\Delta_{m_d}^t(G_d),
\label{eq:zt}
\end{equation}
whereas
\begin{equation}
B\cap Z_T
=\Delta_{\max(1,m_1)}^t(G_1)*\cdots*
 \Delta_{\max(1,m_d)}^t(G_d).
\label{eq:baseline-trace}
\end{equation}
Passing from \eqref{eq:zt} to \eqref{eq:baseline-trace} only replaces a full
simplex at a zero coordinate by the boundary of that simplex. Such a boundary
is nonvoid; for a one-vertex component it is $\{\varnothing\}$, the join
identity. Thus the replacement does not change whether the join is void.

Some $m_j$ is at least $2$. In the nonvoid case,
$\Delta_{m_j}^t(G_j)$ is integer-acyclic and therefore has a nonempty
path-connected realization. Its join with the remaining nonvoid factors is
path-connected. This proves the assertion for $B\cap Z_T$. If all
$m_i\geq1$, then $Z_T=B\cap Z_T$. If some $m_i=0$, then $Z_T$ has a full
simplex factor and is a nonempty cone.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:k-equals-d}
If $k=d$, then $\Delta_d^t(G)\simeq S^{n-d-1}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Enumerate the nonvoid nonbaseline blocks as $X_1,\ldots,X_s$, and let
\begin{equation}
U_0=B,
\qquad
U_j=B\cup X_1\cup\cdots\cup X_j.
\label{eq:successive-unions}
\end{equation}
For each $j$, the intersection $X_j\cap U_{j-1}$ is path-connected. Indeed,
$A_j=X_j\cap B$ is nonvoid and path-connected by
Lemma~\ref{lem:connected-traces}. Each nonvoid pairwise intersection
$X_j\cap X_h$ is also path-connected, and
\[
(X_j\cap X_h)\cap A_j=B\cap X_j\cap X_h
\]
is nonvoid and path-connected. Consequently all pieces of
$X_j\cap U_{j-1}$ are joined through $A_j$.

Every $X_j$ is contractible. Finite subcomplex inclusions are cofibrations, so
the CW-subcomplex form of van Kampen applies to each attachment
$U_j=U_{j-1}\cup X_j$. Since the attaching intersection is path-connected,
$\pi_1(U_j)$ is a quotient of $\pi_1(U_{j-1})$. Therefore, if $q\geq2$, the
initial sphere $U_0=B=S^q$ is simply connected and so is every $U_j$.

Corollary~\ref{cor:total-cut-homology} gives exactly one copy of $\mathbb Z$ in
degree $q$ and no other reduced homology. For $q\geq2$, the wedge criterion
gives $\Delta_d^t(G)\simeq S^q$. If $q=1$, the complex is a connected graph
with first Betti number one and hence is homotopy equivalent to $S^1$. If
$q=0$, it is zero-dimensional with reduced $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$, hence consists
of two points. The case $q=-1$ is $G=dK_1$ and gives
$\{\varnothing\}=S^{-1}$ directly.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem A}

Proposition~\ref{prop:k-equals-d} proves the case $k=d$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:k-d-plus-one} proves $k=d+1$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:k-d-plus-two} proves $k=d+2$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:high-k} proves $k\geq d+3$. These ranges exhaust all
$k\geq d$.

The proof also explains why no single connectivity argument covers all
component counts. The full $2$-skeleton appears only at $k\geq d+3$; at
$k=d+2$ a selected triangular fan suffices; at $k=d+1$ one fills generators
relative to a spanning star; and at $k=d$ one must use the complete
weak-composition cover.

\section{Acyclicity cannot be omitted}\label{sec:counterexample}

The component condition in Theorem A is not merely an artifact of the proof.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:c4-counterexample}
Let $G=C_4\sqcup K_1$ and $d=2$. Then
\begin{equation}
\widetilde H_1(\Delta_2^t(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\widetilde H_2(\Delta_2^t(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\label{eq:c4-homology}
\end{equation}
and all other reduced homology groups vanish. In particular, the conclusion
of Theorem A would predict only $S^2$, so it fails without the component
acyclicity hypothesis.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write the cycle vertices in order as $a,b,c,d$ and let $z$ be the isolated
vertex. The faces not containing $z$ form the boundary of the tetrahedron on
$\{a,b,c,d\}$, hence an $S^2$. A face containing $z$ must leave one of the
opposite independent pairs $\{a,c\}$ or $\{b,d\}$ in its complement.
Therefore the remaining part is
\[
z*(\Delta^{\{a,c\}}\sqcup\Delta^{\{b,d\}}),
\]
attached to the tetrahedral boundary along the two disjoint edges
$L=\Delta^{\{a,c\}}\sqcup\Delta^{\{b,d\}}$. Mayer--Vietoris gives one new
first homology generator while preserving the fundamental $2$-class, which
is exactly \eqref{eq:c4-homology}. More explicitly, the union is the homotopy
cofiber of the inclusion $L\hookrightarrow S^2$. This inclusion is
null-homotopic, so the union is homotopy equivalent to
$S^2\vee\Sigma L\simeq S^2\vee S^1$. Here $\Delta_2^t(C_4)$ is the disjoint
union of two edges and is not integer-acyclic.
\end{proof}

This example does not prove that every individual acyclicity assumption in
Theorem A is necessary. It only shows that one cannot delete all component
topology and retain the same universal formula.

\section{Relation to previous work and scope}\label{sec:scope}

The composition poset, its order-complex calculation, and the
homotopy-colimit model \eqref{eq:hocolim-model} are due to Carnero Bravo
\cite[Proposition 12, Lemma 25, and Corollary 27]{ref2}. Homotopy colimits and
their spectral sequences are standard tools; see \cite{ref5,ref6}. The new use
made here is that objectwise integral acyclicity, rather than objectwise
contractibility, is enough to calculate the integral homology of the global
bounded-independence complex.

The low-component proofs in Section~\ref{sec:connectivity} and the
contractible-factor version of the weak-composition cover first appeared in
the author's preprint \cite{ref8}, which answered \cite[Question 29]{ref2}.
The present Section~\ref{sec:k-equals-d} replaces the former
deformation-retraction argument by connected intersections and van Kampen, so
that the same layer also works under integer acyclicity. The weak-composition
cover is related in spirit to the two-factor decomposition in
\cite[Theorem 34]{ref2} and to polyhedral-join methods \cite{ref7}, but
Proposition~\ref{prop:k-equals-d} is not obtained by iterating the two-factor
theorem: intermediate unions need not retain its null-homotopy hypotheses.

Theorem A subsumes the affirmative answer to \cite[Question 29]{ref2} in
\cite{ref8} and answers \cite[Question 30]{ref2} under the component total-cut
assumptions appearing there. More precisely:
\begin{itemize}
\item it covers every $d\geq2$ and every $k\geq d$;
\item it removes the condition that each $\mathrm{BI}_2(G_i)$ be simply
connected;
\item it replaces ``void or contractible'' by ``void or integer-acyclic'' for
each $\Delta_r^t(G_i)$, $2\leq r\leq d$.
\end{itemize}

It does not classify arbitrary disconnected graphs.
Proposition~\ref{prop:c4-counterexample} shows that arbitrary components can
add homology outside the predicted degree. The paper also does not determine
simple-homotopy type, shellability, torsion when the hypotheses fail, or the
maps between consecutive total-cut filtration levels. No finite computation
is used to prove Theorem A.
